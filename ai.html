Running right now

wade-wiki

A personal wiki that maintains itself.

talk into phone on a walk→transcribed→filed + cross-linked by AI→auto-committed to git

Two layers: raw captures the AI never edits, and a wiki layer it maintains on top. A Mac Mini commits the whole thing to git every two hours. I say something on a walk; by the time I'm home it's filed, linked to five related ideas, and versioned.

It fixes the graveyard problem: ideas you capture and never revisit.

Burpee Counter

Voice counting designed around the AI's failure mode.

Speech recognition mishears a man gasping at rep 70. So spoken numbers set the count instead of incrementing it, and a miss heals itself the next time I say a number. Designing around the model's weakness is most of what AI engineering actually is.

Opinions I've earned

  1. Design around the failure mode, not the demo.

    Self-healing beats occasionally perfect.

  2. Raw stays raw.

    The model never edits originals, only a layer on top that cites them. That one rule is why I trust it with my life's notes.

  3. Files are the treasure. Tools are rentals.

    Plain markdown and git. If the whole stack vanished tomorrow, the knowledge still opens in any text editor.

  4. Autonomy is a dial, not a switch.

    Greenfield at midnight? Full permissions, see you in the morning. Anything I care about? Short leash.

  5. $0 marginal cost is a design constraint that makes you smarter.

    Refusing a metered API forced local transcription and better architecture. The bill stayed flat; the system got smarter.

  6. Specificity beats generality.

    Every AI company is building general. The opportunity is specific.
